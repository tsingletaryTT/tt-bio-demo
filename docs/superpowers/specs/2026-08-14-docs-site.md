# tt-bio docs site — design spec

**Status:** draft for review. Open questions at the end change the build, so they
want answers before implementation.

**One line:** a single-page site that explains what tt-bio is, the research it
stands on, how it came to exist, and what the booth demo shows — written for
someone who has never heard of protein structure prediction.

**Model:** `~/code/tt-local-generator/docs/` — hand-written static HTML, design
tokens in `:root`, `.nojekyll`, served from GitHub Pages at
`docs.tenstorrent.com/<project>/`. Same shape. Different subject and a very
different voice.

---

## 1. What this is for, and what it is not

**Centre of gravity: tt-bio, and the research it is built on.** The booth demo is
the *vehicle*, not the subject. Every section should leave a reader knowing more
about the software and the science; the demo earns its space by making that
concrete.

**The demo's honest claim.** This booth does not discover proteins. It folds five
structures that are already known, on hardware a visitor can see, and shows the
computation happening. Its job is to make "a Tenstorrent card runs
state-of-the-art structural biology" something you have watched rather than
something you have been told. The site should say that plainly — it is a more
interesting claim than the inflated version, and it is the one that survives
contact with a researcher.

### Non-goals

- Not a tutorial for structural biologists. They have the papers.
- Not a benchmark page. Numbers appear where they carry meaning, with their
  conditions attached; there is no leaderboard.
- Not a product pitch for Blackhole. The hardware is interesting here *because*
  of what it was made to run and what it is running instead.

### The hyperbole rule

The user's explicit constraint, and the thing most likely to go wrong. Concrete
rules for whoever writes the copy:

- **No superlatives we cannot source.** "First accurate implementation of a
  state-of-the-art biomolecular structure and binding affinity prediction model
  on accelerators beyond GPUs and TPUs" is a real, attributable claim — it is the
  thesis's own wording, and it should appear *as a quotation with its source*,
  never paraphrased into our own voice as "the first ever".
- **Every number carries its conditions.** "22.3 s" means nothing; "trypsin,
  223 residues, warm, on one Blackhole p300c chip" does.
- **Where tt-bio is slower, say so.** The thesis measures an RTX 4090 at
  1.42–2.18× faster than Blackhole. A page that omits that is not credible about
  anything else on it. The interesting claim was never "faster than a 4090" — it
  is that this class of model runs at all, accurately, on a dataflow architecture,
  and what that costs.
- **No "revolutionary", "game-changing", "unleash", "supercharge".** No em-dash
  drama in the copy where a full stop works.
- **Attribute the science to the scientists.** MIT's Boltz team wrote the models.
  tt-bio ports them. The site must never blur that line.

## 2. Audience

Written for **three readers at once**, in this priority order:

1. **The curious non-expert** — a conference visitor who watched dots become a
   shape and wants to know what they saw. Owns the top third of the page. No
   jargon without an immediate plain-English gloss.
2. **The engineer** — wants to know what runs on what, how fast, and where the
   code is. Owns the middle.
3. **The researcher** — wants to know which models, which checkpoints, what the
   accuracy story is, and what the licence permits. Owns the bottom, and every
   citation.

The page should be readable top-to-bottom by reader 1 without hitting a wall,
and skimmable by readers 2 and 3 straight to their sections.

### Measurements from the thesis (see open question Q2 before publishing)

- Blackhole p150 vs CPU (Ryzen 5 8600G): **3.6× at 128 amino acids → 75.9× at
  1024**.
- **RTX 4090 is 1.42–2.18× faster than Blackhole** across that range; RTX 5090 up
  to 1.46× faster than the 4090 at 1024.
- At 1024 aa, **Triangle Attention 40.2%** and **Triangle Multiplication 26.0%**
  of runtime — both cubic in sequence length. This is the single most useful
  technical fact on the page for an engineer: it says exactly where the cost is
  and therefore what a dataflow architecture has to be good at.

### Measurements from this booth (ours, reproducible)

- Warm folds, one p300c chip: Trp-cage **4.4 s** (20 residues), DNA duplex
  **4.6 s**, FKBP12 **11.7 s**, DHFR **19.7 s**, trypsin **22.3 s**.
- Four chips: **3.70×**, not 4× — two chips throttle to 906 MHz under sustained
  load. Publish the honest number and the reason.
- Two-hour soak: **2,143 folds, zero errors**. Peak 83.6 °C.
- Recorded master: **59 folds across four chips in 150 s**.

the newcomer's questions in the order they actually occur.

| 2 | **What a protein is, and why its shape is the whole problem** | "Why does anyone care?" | 1 |
| 3 | **The five things this booth folds** | "What were those?" — per-protein cards, real images | 1 |
| 4 | **Where the science comes from** | "Who did this?" — AlphaFold 3 → Boltz-1 → Boltz-2 → BoltzGen, credited, cited | 1, 3 |
| 5 | **How tt-bio came to exist** | the story — timeline from the git history + public talks | all |
| 6 | **What tt-bio runs today** | the model table, honestly annotated | 2, 3 |
| 7 | **Why this is interesting on Tenstorrent** | dataflow, and the Triangle Attention/Multiplication cost centre | 2 |
| 8 | **What the demo actually does** | the booth: live trajectory, confidence colour, four chips, the quad | 1, 2 |
| 9 | **The easter eggs** | rewards the curious; see §6 | 1 |
| 10 | **Try it / read it / cite it** | repo links, papers, licence, the talks | 2, 3 |

### Section 2 is the one that has to be perfect

This is where a non-expert either stays or leaves. Draft direction — plain, short
sentences, no analogy that overclaims:

> A protein is a chain of amino acids. The chain folds into a specific
> three-dimensional shape, and that shape decides what the protein does — what it
> binds to, what it switches on, what a drug can grip.
>
> The sequence is easy to read. The shape is hard to find, and for fifty years
> finding it meant months of laboratory work per structure. Predicting the shape
> from the sequence was one of biology's long-standing open problems, and the
> progress made on it with deep learning was recognised with the 2024 Nobel Prize
> in Chemistry.
>
> The dots you are watching are a model working out that shape. They start as
> noise and are pulled, step by step, into a structure.

Then the confidence-colour explainer, which is the demo's most honest feature:
the model says how sure it is, residue by residue, and the booth colours it —
orange where it is guessing, blue where it is confident. **Point at the DNA
duplex being orange throughout.** A demo that shows its own uncertainty is worth
more than one that does not.

### Section 5: the story, told from evidence

Beats, each anchored to a source in §3:

1. A computer science student at TU Munich finds the Tenstorrent community.
   ("From Joining the Discord to Drug Discovery.")
2. **New Year's Day 2025** — first commits: `add tenstorrent accelerator`. The
   date is real and it is a good detail. Do not embellish it.
3. Berlin, AI Plumbers: he presents *AlphaFold 3 on Tenstorrent Wormhole* to the
   community that got him started.
4. A TUM thesis, *Porting Boltz-2 to Tenstorrent Accelerators*, October 2025.
5. He joins Tenstorrent (**see Q1 — needs his consent and a public source**).
6. **2026-06-06:** the project outgrows its own name. Quote the commit.
7. Today: nine model families, v0.6.2, 1,744 commits by one person.

Tone: understated. The facts carry it. Resist "incredible journey".

## 5. Design direction

Not a clone of tt-local-generator's palette. That site is about *generated
images* and leans neon-on-black; this one is about *structures and measurement*
and should feel closer to an instrument than a poster.

**Ground it in the subject.** The palette is already decided by the application,
and reusing it is what ties page to booth:

- **Base** `#092221` — the brand's near-black teal, and the booth's own ground.
- **Accent** `#1B8EB1` — links, buttons.
- **The confidence ramp is the page's colour system**, not decoration:
  `#0053D6` (>90) · `#65CBF3` (70–90) · `#FFDB13` (50–70) · `#FF7D45` (<50).
  Use it for section accents, table emphasis, and the protein cards — so that by
  the time a reader reaches the explainer, they have already been reading in that
  vocabulary. This is the one genuinely distinctive design idea here and it
  should be carried consistently.
- **Neutrals** biased toward the base's teal, never pure grey.

**Type.** Neue Haas Unica Pro (body) and Degular Display (display) are the brand
faces; **Berkeley Mono** for every number, sequence, and command. The CSP blocks
font CDNs, so either inline as `@font-face` data URIs or fall back to a declared
system stack — a silent fallback is worse than a chosen one.

**Layout.** Single column, ~65 characters of running text, full-bleed only for
the video and the quad screenshot. Tables scroll inside their own
`overflow-x: auto`. Both themes defined at token level (see the artifact/CSP
rules in the global guidance): complete light palette on bare `:root`, dark
redefined under `prefers-color-scheme` guarded against an explicit light choice,
and again under `[data-theme="dark"]`.

**Motion, used once.** One idea, executed well: a small canvas that runs the
*actual* condensation — a cloud of points pulled into a structure — as the hero
background or beside section 2. We already have the maths (`ui/mark.py`'s
gradient descent on a signed distance field). It is the page's thesis in one
animation: noise becomes shape. Everything else stays still. Respect
`prefers-reduced-motion`.

**No stock imagery, no AI-generated illustration.** Every image is either the
running application (we have current screenshots) or a real structure.

## 6. The easter eggs

The user asked for these to be shown, and they earn their place because both are
*honest about what they are*:

- **`Ctrl+G` — the Tenstorrent mark.** A cloud of Gaussian noise pulled into the
  logo by gradient descent on a signed distance field, computed **on the chips**
  in ttnn, streamed back over the same protocol a fold uses, and varying each
  run. The booth labels it "Not a fold" precisely because it is not one. That
  self-labelling is the point worth writing about: the demo's credibility comes
  from refusing to let a pretty thing pass as science.
- **The DNA duplex** — the only thing the booth folds that is not a protein, and
  it comes out orange (low confidence) because a twelve-rung duplex is exactly
  what the model is least sure about. Reward for the curious, and a second
  chance to make the confidence point.

Presentation: a "things we hid in it" section, low-key, near the bottom. Not the
lede.

## 7. Technical shape

- **Hand-written static HTML/CSS**, one `index.html`, no build step, no
  framework. Matches the prior art and survives being handed to anyone.
- **`docs/` + `.nojekyll`**, GitHub Pages.
- Assets reuse `docs/screenshots/` — already current (taglines, legend, DNA,
  quad, the 1080p60 loop).
- Self-contained: no CDN, no external font, no analytics. The venue is offline
  and the page should work there too.
- OG/Twitter cards pointing at the quad screenshot, per the prior art.
- Page weight budget: **under 12 MB** including the 9.4 MB video. If that is too
  heavy, link the video rather than inlining a second copy.

## 8. Open questions — these change the build

**Q1 — How much do we say about Moritz personally?** His talks, thesis, GitHub
and X posts are public and citable. His *employment* I could only confirm from
internal sources (a Slack welcome dated 2025-12-08; an HR training tracker
listing "Sr. Field Application Engineer"). **Recommendation: do not publish the
role or start date from internal records.** Either get Moritz's OK and a public
source, or write the beat as "later joined Tenstorrent" with no date — the story
works without it. He should see the section before it ships either way.

**Q2 — May we publish the thesis numbers?** The 3.6×/75.9× CPU speedups, the
RTX comparisons and the Triangle-Attention breakdown are the most technically
substantial content available, but the thesis is an internal PDF and I found no
public copy. **Recommendation: ask Moritz whether the thesis can be linked
publicly.** If yes, cite it and use the numbers freely. If no, cut them and lean
on the booth's own measurements, which are ours to publish.

**Q3 — Whose site is this, and where does it live?** The subject is tt-bio, which
is `moritztng/tt-bio` — not ours. The demo is ours. Options:
  - (a) publish from `tt-bio-demo/docs/` as *the booth demo's* site, with tt-bio
    as its subject and heavy outbound linking. Honest, and we control it.
    **Recommended.**
  - (b) offer it upstream as tt-bio's own site — better home for the content,
    but it is Moritz's repo and his call.
  I would build (a) and offer (b).

**Q4 — Is a 2024 Nobel Prize mention right?** It is accurate and it is the
fastest way to tell a newcomer this field matters. But AlphaFold is DeepMind's;
Boltz is a separate, open reimplementation lineage. Suggested handling: mention
the prize as *context for the problem*, in the same breath as making clear Boltz
is independent work by MIT. Flagging because it is exactly the sentence that
could read as borrowed glory if written carelessly.

**Q5 — Does the site cover BoltzGen and drug design?** FOSDEM's abstract puts
binder design front and centre, and it is the most consequential thing tt-bio
enables — but the booth does not demo it. **Recommendation: cover it in section
6 as "what tt-bio does that this booth does not show", explicitly.** That keeps
the booth's claim narrow while letting the software's real reach be stated.
